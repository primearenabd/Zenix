## Ranking videos

### Ads

#### R1. Which ad should a supermarket run first?

*Ranking of five ad types, by how close each is to a shopper walking in.*

**Script**

> Which ad should a supermarket run first? I ranked five, by how close each one is to a shopper walking in. Fifth: a brand video. Nice, but hard to measure. Fourth: a recipe ad. It builds trust, but sells slowly. Third: a fresh produce video. Second: a directions ad for people nearby. First: the weekly hero deal, with the price and the address. Comment ADS and Zenix will send you the guide.

#### R2. Your ad isn't working. What do you fix first?

*Ranking of five things to fix, in order.*

**Script**

> Your ad is not working. What do you fix first? I ranked five. Fifth: the budget. More money into a weak ad is still a weak ad. Fourth: the placement. Third: the audience. Keep it to people near the store. Second: the picture or video. First: the offer. If nobody can tell what is on sale, nothing else matters. Comment FIX and Zenix will send you the guide.

## Comparison videos

### Ads

#### C1. Boost a post or run a proper ad?

*Which should a supermarket use. Decision rule.*

**Script**

> Boost a post or run a proper ad: which should a supermarket use? Boost is quick. You pick a post, a budget and an audience, and go. A proper ad gives you more control over the goal and who sees it, but it takes longer to set up. If you are new and have one clear offer, start with a boost. If you want more control, move to a proper ad. Either way, fix the offer first. Comment BOOST and Zenix will send you the guide.

#### C2. Google Ads or Meta Ads?

*Which should a supermarket use. Decision rule.*

**Script**

> Google Ads or Meta Ads for a supermarket? Google shows your ad to people already searching, like grocery store near me. Meta shows your offer to people scrolling, so it suits this week's deals. If people search for what you sell, try Google. If you want to show a deal, try Meta. Start with one, and measure store visits, calls or messages. Comment CHOOSE and Zenix will send you the guide.

#### C3. Organic posts or paid ads?

*Where should a new supermarket start. Decision rule.*

**Script**

> Organic posts or paid ads: where should a new supermarket start? Organic is free, but it is slow if few people follow you. Ads reach people nearby faster, but they need a clear offer, or you pay to be ignored. If you have no followers and one strong offer, try a small local ad. If your offer is unclear, fix that first with organic posts. Comment START and Zenix will send you the guide.

### Social media growth

#### C4. Facebook or Instagram for this week's deal?

*Where should the deal go first. Decision rule.*

**Script**

> Facebook or Instagram: where should this week's deal go first? Do not guess. Open your insights and look at where more of your local followers react. Post the deal there first, then copy it to the other. A deal people can read in one second works on both. Comment WHERE and Zenix will send you the guide.

#### C5. Run your own social media or hire help?

*Who should run it. Decision rule.*

**Script**

> Should a supermarket run its own social media or hire help? Do it yourself if you have a few hours a week and a simple routine. Hire help if you have the budget but not the time, or if your posts keep stopping after two weeks. Either way, one person has to own it. Comment HELP and Zenix will send you the guide.

### Local SEO and Google

#### C6. Google Business Profile or Facebook Page?

*Which does a supermarket need first. Decision rule.*

**Script**

> Google Business Profile or Facebook Page: which does a supermarket need first? The Google profile. It is where people look when they want a store right now: hours, directions, the phone number. A Facebook Page is where your community talks to you. Start with Google, then build the page. Comment PROFILE and Zenix will send you the guide.

#### C7. Local SEO or social media?

*Where should a new supermarket start. Decision rule.*

**Script**

> Local SEO or social media: where should a new supermarket start? Local SEO gets you found by people who are ready to shop. Social media helps people remember you and come back. A new store needs to be found first. So fix your Google profile this week, then post weekly deals. Comment LOCAL and Zenix will send you the guide.

### Tools

*Two posters for the same store and deals. Viewers vote.*

**Script**

> Same store, same deals, two posters. Poster A has twenty offers. Poster B has one hero deal and two supporting ones. Look at both for two seconds. Which one can you remember? That is the test, and I would bet on B. Comment A or B. Then comment POSTER and Zenix will send you the template.

#### C9. Meta Business Suite or Buffer?

*Which should a supermarket use to schedule posts. Confirm each tool's current platforms and free plan before filming.*

**Script**

> Meta Business Suite or Buffer: which one should a supermarket use to schedule posts? Both have a free way to start. Meta Business Suite is built for Facebook and Instagram. Buffer is cleaner to use and covers more platforms. If you only post on Facebook and Instagram, start with Meta. If you also want other platforms, try Buffer. Comment SCHEDULE and Zenix will send you the comparison.

## Hook bank: 24 spoken hooks that keep showing up in the research

How I found them: I read published hook guides and creator tests for short-form video from 2026. Several of them analyse real videos (one reports 3,600 TikToks across six niches, another tests 30 hooks), but they are vendor and creator blogs, not independent studies, and their percentages are not verified. I kept the patterns that appear across several sources and ignored the numbers. Two of them stand out: negative or contrarian framings and curiosity-gap openings scored best in more than one write-up. Treat all of it as a place to start testing, not a guarantee.

Spoken hooks work best at about 10 to 14 words, landing in the first 1 to 3 seconds, with the main idea in the first sentence. The two graphics you sent are lists of exactly these patterns, so I included their lines.

**Needs proof** means the hook is only honest if the claim is true. Do not use it without evidence.
