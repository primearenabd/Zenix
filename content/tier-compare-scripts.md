# Zenix Reels: Ranking and Comparison Scripts (Tools Edition)

Three finished scripts for ranking and comparing real tools, plus the analysis behind them. No clip prompts yet, by design: scripts first.
Status: draft. Placements marked DRAFT must be confirmed by testing each tool before filming.

---

## What was wrong with the first tier list

The first TIER script ranked six post types. It was opinion only, with nothing to see, nothing to test and no tool to try. Ranking and comparison reels work when the viewer gets a verdict they can act on today. So the new rule: **rank real tools, for one specific job, using criteria stated on screen, from a test we actually ran.**

## The format (every ranking or comparison reel)

1. **A question hook:** "What's the best free tool for X?" The viewer wants the answer.
2. **One job and three criteria, said in one line.** It makes the ranking feel fair and stops "but what about Y" comments.
3. **Progressive reveal:** worst to best, with a short reason for each (one fact, not a feature list).
4. **One placement people can disagree with.** Comments drive sends.
5. **A decision rule in comparisons:** "If you do X, pick A. If you do Y, pick B."
6. **Comment keyword and a guide** with the full table or template.

## Script checks (run on every script)

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| Does the hook ask a question the target viewer already has? | Stops the scroll |
| Is the job and the criteria stated in one line? | Makes the verdict credible |
| Is every tool fact either tested by us or confirmed on the tool's own page? | Avoids false claims |
| Is there no price, limit or plan detail that can go out of date? | Prices and free limits change, and my sources disagreed |
| Is there one placement worth arguing about? | Comments and sends |
| Does the ending give a rule or a next step? | Saves |
| Does the keyword match a finished guide? | Delivery |

**Facts I could not pin down:** search results disagreed on Canva's paid price and on whether CapCut's free plan puts a watermark on exports, and they are vendor blogs. So the scripts state no prices, no limits and no watermark claims. Check each tool's own plan page the day you film.

## Test plan (do this before filming, about 10 minutes per tool)

For the poster reel, build the same poster in each tool: one hero deal, two supporting offers, store name and hours. Screen-record each build. Note three things per tool: time to a finished export, whether the free plan let you export without a paywall or watermark, and how much of the result looks good without a designer. The recordings are your b-roll and your proof. If a result contradicts a DRAFT placement below, change the placement, not the facts.

# Script 1: POSTER (tier list)

**Hook (spoken, and on screen):** "What's the best free tool for a supermarket's weekly poster? I made the same poster in three."
**Graphic:** a three-row tier list. Screen recordings of each tool build play in the top half as it is revealed.

**Script:**
"What's the best free tool for a supermarket's weekly poster? I made the same poster in three. I ranked them on three things: free to start, time to finish, and no designer needed. Third: PosterMyWall. Fast, but its layouts lean toward events. Second: Adobe Express. A free plan with AI credits, but fewer strong templates. First: Canva. A big free template library and a clean export. Comment POSTER and Zenix will send you the template."

**DRAFT placements:** Canva first, Adobe Express second, PosterMyWall third. Each reason comes from a third-party write-up and must be confirmed on screen during your test. Replace any reason your test does not support.
**Arguable placement:** second and third are close. Invite disagreement in the caption: "Which would you pick?"
**Guide to send:** the poster template plus the three-step fix (hero deal, two supporting offers, store details).
**Keyword:** POSTER

# Script 2: RESTAURANT (tier list, ranked by what to set up first)

**Hook:** "What's the best free marketing tool for a restaurant? I ranked four by what to set up first."
**Graphic:** a four-row tier list, C at the bottom to S at the top, each tool's logo or screen recording as it drops in.

**Script:**
"What's the best free marketing tool for a restaurant? I ranked four by what to set up first. C tier: an email tool like Mailchimp. Great later, not first. B tier: a scheduler like Buffer. It saves time once you have something to post. A tier: Canva. Good-looking posts without a designer. S tier: your Google Business Profile. It is free, and nearby people find you there. Comment TOOLS and Zenix will send you the guide."

**Why this ranking is defensible:** it is ordered by sequence (what to do first), not by feature count, so it does not compare unlike tools on quality. A Google Business Profile is free and is how nearby customers find a business, which is why it tops the list. Third-party restaurant guides agree it is the place to start.
**Arguable placement:** email at C. Invite: "Would you move it up?"
**Guide to send:** a one-page setup order: profile, then posts, then scheduling, then email.
**Keyword:** TOOLS

# Script 3: SCHEDULE (comparison)

**Hook:** "Meta Business Suite or Buffer: which one should a supermarket use to schedule posts?"
**Graphic:** a split screen with both tools side by side. Two rows appear as spoken: "Facebook and Instagram only" and "More platforms".

**Script:**
"Meta Business Suite or Buffer: which one should a supermarket use to schedule posts? Both have a free way to start. Meta Business Suite is built for Facebook and Instagram. Buffer is cleaner to use and covers more platforms. If you only post on Facebook and Instagram, start with Meta. If you also want other platforms, try Buffer. Comment SCHEDULE and Zenix will send you the comparison."

**To confirm when filming:** the platforms each tool supports today, and what its free plan includes. Say only what is on each tool's own page that day.
**Arguable point:** "cleaner to use" is our opinion. Say it as one.
**Guide to send:** a one-page comparison table and a weekly posting schedule.
**Keyword:** SCHEDULE

## Risks to check before each one

- **Stay within the free plan** and say so on screen. If a feature needs a paid plan, say that too. Do not state prices.
- **AI food images:** if you test AI image tools, never present generated produce or products as real stock photos or as the actual item on sale.
- **Email and SMS tools:** if you later test them, marketing messages need customer consent, and text messaging has its own rules in the U.S. Do not suggest sending to people who have not opted in.
- **Real data:** when you record, blur customer names, phone numbers, account IDs and any keys.
- **QR and link tools:** check whether the free plan adds ads, expires or lets you edit the link later.
- **Use the tool's current screens,** and re-test if a feature looks different from what a guide says.
